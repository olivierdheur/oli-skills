# Winners

Your best-performing posts, one file per post, sorted into five buckets by format. The writing skills read these to copy your patterns: the hook shapes, structures and topics that already worked for your audience. They copy patterns, never words.

Winners beat any generic example. Until a bucket has winners, its skills fall back to their own starter examples.

---

## Buckets

| Folder | What goes in it | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `short-form-video/` | Reels, TikToks, Shorts, any vertical talking-head or b-roll video | `short-form-video-*` skills |
| `short-form-text/` | Single tweets, Threads posts, Substack Notes | `short-form-text-*` skills |
| `long-form-text/` | Full LinkedIn posts, long X posts | `long-form-text-*` skills |
| `newsletter/` | Newsletter issues, Substack posts, X Articles | `newsletter-*` skills |
| `visual/` | Carousels, infographics, document posts | `visual-*` skills |

If a post could fit two buckets, file it where its format did the work. A video that won on its on-screen text is still short-form video.

## How a winner gets here

- `content-wins` pulls your post stats, picks the posts that beat your own usual numbers, and files them after you approve each one.
- Or add one by hand: copy `_template.md` into the right bucket, fill it in, and add a line to that bucket's `README.md`.

## Naming and format

- File name: `YYYY-MM-DD-<short-slug>.md`, using the date it was posted.
- Frontmatter and sections: see `_template.md`.
- Each bucket's `README.md` lists every winner in one line, newest first. Skills read that index, then open only the 3–5 winners closest to the task, so the folder can grow without slowing anything down.

## What counts as a winner

Judge a post against your own account, not against other creators. A post is a winner when it clearly beats your usual numbers on the metric you care about most (see "Metrics you care about" in `creator-ikigai.md`). Saves, shares and follows usually say more than views.

Keep the "Why it won" section honest. If you don't know why, say so; a guess labeled as a guess is still useful.
