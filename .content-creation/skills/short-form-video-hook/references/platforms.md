# Platforms: hooks for Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts

The method is the same on all three: a spoken hook in the first ~3 seconds plus an on-screen text hook that works with the sound off. What changes is what sits around the hook and what each platform rewards. Checked against official help docs, November 2026. Platforms change these often.

## What's the same everywhere

- Vertical 9:16 video, full screen. The first frame and first sentence decide whether the viewer stays.
- Many people watch with the sound off, so the visual hook has to carry the idea on its own.
- The platform's buttons and caption cover the bottom and right edge of the screen. Put the text overlay in the upper-middle of the frame so nothing covers it.
- Each platform can show the spoken words as auto-captions. Creators commonly report that platforms also use on-screen text and speech to understand the topic, so saying the topic's keyword out loud in the hook may help search.

## Instagram Reels

- **What sits next to the hook:** the cover frame (shown on the profile grid) and the first line of the caption, which is cut off after a short preview. Make the cover readable on its own; it's often the visual hook.
- **What it rewards:** Instagram has said sends (shares by DM) and watch time are among the strongest signals for reaching new people. Hooks that make the viewer think of a specific friend do well here, which is why the Shareability Score matters most on Reels.
- **Originality:** Instagram has said it shows reposted content and videos with other platforms' watermarks less in recommendations. Upload a clean file, not a TikTok download.
- **Length:** Reels can run up to 20 minutes, but only Reels of 3 minutes or less are recommended to people who don't follow the account. The hook's job doesn't change with length.

## TikTok

- **What sits next to the hook:** the caption and any on-screen text sticker. TikTok is used as a search engine, so a hook that names the problem in the words people search for works twice.
- **What it rewards:** watch time and completion, rewatches, shares and comments. A hook that opens a loop the video closes at the end suits TikTok well.
- **Length:** the in-app camera records up to 10 minutes, and uploads can run longer. Only videos at least 1 minute long are eligible for TikTok's Creator Rewards Program, so if the user is chasing that, the script, not the hook, has to stretch.

## YouTube Shorts

- **What sits next to the hook:** the title, shown over the video in the Shorts player. Treat the title as a second visual hook: short, specific, same promise. Titles are capped at 100 characters.
- **What it rewards:** YouTube Studio shows "viewed vs. swiped away" for Shorts, which is effectively a direct score for the hook. Retention and replays matter after that.
- **Length:** Shorts can run up to 3 minutes. A Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim (for example, copyrighted music) is blocked.
- **Links:** links in Shorts descriptions and comments aren't clickable, so a hook that promises "the link" needs a different payoff on Shorts (a pinned related video, or "it's on my channel").

## Posting the same video to all three

- One visual hook usually works on all three. Check that the overlay isn't covered by any platform's buttons.
- Rewrite the text around it per platform: a cover for Reels, a searchable caption for TikTok, a title for Shorts.
- A hook that promises a DM ("comment X and I'll send it") depends on the user having a way to send DMs at scale on that platform. That's easy to set up on Instagram, harder elsewhere (check the user's setup); adjust the promise in the hook if needed.
