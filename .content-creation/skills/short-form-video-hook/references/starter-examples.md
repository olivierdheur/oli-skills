# Starter examples: short-form video hooks

Use these only until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/short-form-video/`. Once they do, their winners outrank everything here.

Copy the **pattern and the template**, never the words. Every example is a real published hook, credited to its creator. Swap in the user's niche, the user's authority sources and the user's reader, then run the Originality Test.

---

## Patterns from published hooks (@its.mikareyes)

### 1. Authority reframe: "[Big name] says the next [big thing] isn't [expected]. It's this instead."
**Type:** AUTHORITY
**Why it works:** a high-authority name lends borrowed trust; "not X, it's Y" opens a curiosity gap the viewer has to close.

- **Spoken template:** `[High-authority person or firm] says the next [big opportunity] is not [what people think]. It's [reveal] instead.`
- **On-screen template:** `[Authority]: This is the next $[X] [category] (not [expected])` or `[Authority]: DON'T chase [common goal]`

Examples (strong saves):
- Spoken: "The investors behind Airbnb, Stripe, and Google said that the next trillion dollar company is not going to be software. It's going to be this instead." / On-screen: "Sequoia: This is the next $1 TRILLION dollar company (not software)"
- Spoken: "The billionaire Shark Tank shark, Mark Cuban, says do not chase traditional tech jobs. Chase this instead." / On-screen: "Mark Cuban: DON'T chase tech jobs"

### 2. Surprising salary at a known company
**Type:** AUTHORITY (the company's own careers page is the source)
**Why it works:** a specific high salary at a recognizable company is instantly comparable; viewers measure their own pay against it in one second. A screenshot overlay does the proof, so the spoken claim doesn't read as hype.

- **Spoken template:** `[These jobs / This role] [is/are] getting paid more than [expected top earner] at [well-known company].`
- **On-screen template:** a screenshot of the source page with the unexpected number highlighted.

Example (strong shares):
- Spoken: "These jobs are getting paid more than product managers on Anthropic's Career Page." / On-screen: screenshot of the careers page with the salary range highlighted.

### 3. "Fire your [expert]" workflow replacement
**Type:** TUTORIAL
**Why it works:** "fire your X" is identity-aggressive; it makes the viewer defend or abandon an expert they pay for. A logo stack of connected tools proves capability visually so it doesn't feel like hype. Running it as a numbered series builds return viewers.

- **Spoken template:** `It's time to fire your [expert role] and replace them with [tool].`
- **On-screen template:** `Fire your [expert] for a [tool] [role]` + a logo stack of the connected tools.

Example (high saves and comments):
- Spoken: "It's time to fire your personal trainer and replace them with Claude." / On-screen: "Fire your personal trainer for a Claude Health Coach" + Oura, Strava, Apple Health and Apple Fitness logos.

### 4. Single-authority blind spot + fix
**Type:** AUTHORITY
**Why it works:** one institution both exposes an invisible flaw in *the viewer's own* habit and ships the fix, so the credibility and the payoff come from the same trusted source. The second-person "if you do X" makes the stat personal; quantifying the fix ("4-prompt") makes it feel finite and copyable.

- **Spoken template:** `[Institution] just proved that if you [common behavior], you have a [blind spot]. So they built a [N-step] fix that [catches what you're missing].`
- **On-screen template:** `[Institution] proved [behavior] has a [X%] blind spot (& fixed it)`

Example (37% skip rate, strong saves and comments):
- Spoken: "Stanford just proved that if you use AI for research, you have a blind spot. So they built a 4-prompt fix that catches everything you're missing." / On-screen: "Stanford proved AI research has a 60% blind spot (& fixed it)"

### 5. Dual authority: one names the problem, one has the fix
**Type:** AUTHORITY
**Why it works:** two authorities doing different jobs in one hook. The institution makes the problem unarguable; the named person makes the fix aspirational. A relatable label for the problem ("yes man") lands instantly.

- **Spoken template:** `[Institution] just proved [tool/habit] is [relatable flaw]. [Stat]. And [named person, one-line credential] built the fix.`
- **On-screen template:** `[Institution]: exposed [problem] / [Person]: I have a fix`

Example (a 58x outlier on the account; one credential clause trimmed from the original):
- Spoken: "It's official. Stanford just proved Claude is a yes man. It agrees with you 49% more than a human would. And Andrej Karpathy, founding member of OpenAI, built the fix." / On-screen: "Stanford: exposed AI's problem / Karpathy: I have a fix"

## Patterns seen across outlier creators (no wording copied)

Recurring shapes from high-performing videos in the AI-for-non-technical niche. Templates only.

- **Authority + insider artifact.** `[Authority] just [proved/dropped] [finding + stat]. Here's [the prompt / file / N things] to fix it this week.` A credentialed source plus a small copyable artifact promises an easy rescue.
- **"Replace your entire [expensive team]".** `Did you know you can run your entire [costly team] inside [tool]? Before you spend [thousands / hours] on [task], try this.` Anchoring on a recognizable expensive department lets the viewer mentally cancel a bill.
- **Fear stat + one named fix.** `Did you know [tool] [fails] [X–Y%] of the time? There's one [fix] that stops it.`
- **"Stop [old way]. [New way] instead."** Declare a skill the viewer thinks they need obsolete, attribute the shift to a named insider, prove it with a screenshot of their post, then break the new way into a small numbered set. If part 1 goes viral, a part 2 ("so many of you asked for this") can come close to the same numbers.
- **Invisible problem.** `The longer you [habit], the worse [outcome] gets, and most people don't notice it happening. Here's how to catch it.`
- **Surprising lucrative skill + negation.** `This [skill] is paying [specific high $] right now, and it's not [obvious answer].` The negation is the open loop; a quick stack of named recent news sources is the credibility.

Cross-pattern observations:
- Borrowed authority (a named institution, researcher or company) is the most common edge among outliers: they sell access to insider knowledge, not their own expertise.
- Specific numbers stack well: a dollar figure, a time saved, a percentage.
- One named tool as the protagonist beats a generic "AI".
- A "comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [the asset]" CTA shows up on most of them. That belongs in the script and caption, not the hook.
