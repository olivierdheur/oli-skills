# Platforms: long-form posts on LinkedIn and X

The method is the same on both: one payload, built to be saved, 250–400 words. What changes is the limit, the fold, formatting and what each feed rewards. Checked against official help docs, November 2026. Platforms change these often.

## LinkedIn

- **Length limit.** 3,000 characters per post. A 400-word post is usually 2,200–2,600 characters, so the 250–400 word range fits with room to spare.
- **The fold.** Roughly the first two to three lines show before "…see more" (commonly cited as about 200 characters on desktop, fewer on mobile). The hook has to land there; see `long-form-text-hook`.
- **Formatting.** Plain text. There's no native bold or italics; "bold" Unicode characters from converter tools are read badly by screen readers and search, so skip them. Line breaks and blank lines are kept. Dashes and numbers for lists.
- **Links.** Creators commonly report that posts with an external link in the body get less reach. That's why the skill allows one link at most and moves extras to the first comment.
- **Hashtags and tags.** Optional, and a few at most. Tagging companies pulls readers off the post.
- **What it rewards.** LinkedIn's engineering team has said its feed ranking uses dwell time; creators commonly report that saves and comments from the poster's network also drive distribution. A post that holds the reader long enough to work through a checklist fits that well.

## Long X posts

- **Length limit.** 280 characters for standard accounts. Paid (Premium) accounts can post up to 25,000 characters. Without a paid account, post the long version on LinkedIn only.
- **The fold.** The timeline cuts long posts with "Show more", commonly reported at around the first 280 characters. The hook and its proof have to sit above that cut.
- **Formatting.** Paid accounts can use bold and italics in long posts. Default to plain text anyway so the same draft works on LinkedIn; add emphasis only if the user asks.
- **Links.** Creators commonly report that posts with external links get less reach. Put the link in a reply to the post if reach matters more than clicks.
- **What it rewards.** Creators commonly report that replies, reposts and bookmarks, especially in the first hour, drive reach. Bookmarks map directly to the save score.
- **Tone.** Blunter and faster than LinkedIn. Cut warm-up sentences, and keep the setup to two lines or fewer.

## Posting the same piece on both

- Write the LinkedIn version first; it's the stricter on formatting.
- For X, cut the setup to its shortest form and check that the claim and proof sit in the first 280 characters.
- Keep the payload identical. It's the part that earns the save on either platform.
