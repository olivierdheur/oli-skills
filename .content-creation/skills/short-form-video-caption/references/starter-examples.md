# Starter examples: short-form video captions

Used until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/short-form-video/`. Once they do, the **Caption** section of their winners beats everything here.

Two parts:
- **A real caption** by Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes), credited, with the moves labeled.
- **Illustrations (not real posts)** for the two longer archetypes. The creators are fictional, and so are their numbers and stories.

Copy the structure, never the words, and never borrow a number. On content, the augment rule in `SKILL.md` still wins: keep the shape, but fill it with what the user's video didn't say.

## Real caption

### Authority Rules: "The N rules from [authority]"
Credit: Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes). The shortest form, about 60 words.

```
comment "trim" and I'll send you my Context Cleanup Checklist ⚡

Or "challenge" to join my AI Challenge :)

The 3 new rules from Anthropic:
1. Give AI the outcome you want, not more rules.
2. Load context only when it's actually needed.
3. Show an example instead of describing it.

ai tips, ai prompts, ai tools for beginners, Claude, Anthropic, OpenAI, ChatGPT, how do you cleanup Ai context?, what is context rot
```

**Why it works:**
- **Two CTAs, both first.** A comment keyword for the lead magnet, then a second offer on its own line for the higher-intent reader. Two doors, no extra words.
- **Borrowed authority does the setup in six words.** The source is the credibility; the number is the promise.
- **One line per rule, imperative voice.** Each one is an instruction the reader can act on without the video. The terseness is the format.
- **No closer.** The rules end and the caption stops.
- **Casual punctuation left alone.** The ⚡, the `:)`, the lowercase keyword line, the "Ai" typo-casing in a question. That's the tell a human wrote it.
- **No 🏷️ prefix** on a caption this short; the keyword line still runs concepts, proper nouns, then literal questions.

**Augment version of the same shape.** If the video already stated the three rules, the caption lists how to apply each one instead:

```
How to use the 3 rules today:
1. Swap your 10-line rule list for one sentence describing the finished result.
2. Move reference docs into a file the AI opens only when the task needs it.
3. Paste one past output you liked and say "match this."
```

## Illustrations (not real posts)

### The fictional creators

- **Dana Okafor**, a solo bookkeeper with 30 small-business clients and no assistant. Audience: freelancers drowning in admin. Uses a comment keyword, `INTAKE`.
- **Leo Marsh**, who runs a two-location coffee shop with his sister. Audience: small-business owners who think AI is for tech companies. No keyword yet, so he uses his follow line.

Neither creator has approved any tags, so every tool appears as a plain name.

### Build Stack: "I built X in Y hours, here's the stack and the process"
Script type: TUTORIAL. The video showed the finished client intake form and said it took an afternoon. The caption adds the stack, the order of work and the gotcha.

```
Comment "INTAKE" and I'll send you the exact form questions! (make sure you're following so I can message you!)

I replaced 45 minutes of back-and-forth emails per new client with one form I built in an afternoon, no developer.

What I used:

📝 Typeform for the questions, with logic that skips the payroll section if they have no employees
🔗 Zapier to drop every answer into a new client folder
✍️ ChatGPT to turn the answers into my welcome email draft

How to build yours:

1️⃣ Write down every question you asked your last three clients by email

2️⃣ Cut anything you can look up yourself (company address, tax ID format)

3️⃣ Build the form and send it to one existing client before going live. Mine found two confusing questions in five minutes.

4️⃣ Connect it to your folders so nothing lives in your inbox

The tools did the plumbing. I still decide which clients are a fit.

🏷️ Typeform, Zapier, ChatGPT, client onboarding, bookkeeping business, small business automation, freelancer admin, how to automate client onboarding, client intake form template, how do i stop emailing clients for documents, what should a client intake form ask
```

**Why it works:** the setup line carries a number (45 minutes) and a constraint (no developer) the video didn't say. Each tool line says what the tool *did*, not just its name. Step 3 carries the behind-the-scenes gotcha. The closer is an ownership line.

### Numbered Roster: "The N things that run my X"
Script type: TUTORIAL. The video listed four AI helpers by name. The caption adds the cadence and the human detail for each.

```
Follow for more ways to run a small business without working weekends.

Here are the 4 AI helpers that run the back office of our two coffee shops. Each one is just a saved prompt or a scheduled automation, nothing custom-built.

1. Rota writer. Every Thursday it turns staff availability into next week's schedule. My sister checks it over coffee and changes maybe two shifts.

2. Review replier. Every morning it drafts replies to new online reviews. I rewrite the angry ones myself.

3. Order checker. Every Sunday it compares milk and bean orders against last month's sales. We stopped throwing out oat milk.

4. Menu describer. Whenever we add a drink, it writes the menu card and the post. I just pick the photo.

If you're starting from zero, start with the review replier. It takes ten minutes to set up and you'll feel it the first morning.

🏷️ ChatGPT, AI for small business, coffee shop owner, small business automation, restaurant management, how to use AI in a coffee shop, how to reply to reviews with AI, how do small businesses use AI, can AI make a staff schedule
```

**Why it works:** the labels teach on their own; a skimmer still gets the idea. Every item runs cadence → what it does → a human detail. The demystify line turns "AI helpers" into something ordinary. The closer is a start-here line.

## What doesn't ship

- The script read back as a list. If the video named the three tools, the caption says what each one did, or the setting that made it work.
- Hashtags, or a keyword line padded with tools the video never mentioned.
- A handle the user didn't approve, or a guessed one.
- A setup line that repeats the video's hook.
- A borrowed credential ("while I work a full-time job") that isn't the user's.
