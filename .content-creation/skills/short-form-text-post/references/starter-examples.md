# Starter examples: short-form text

Use these only until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/short-form-text/`. Copy the **move** (structure, rhythm, how a line turns), never the words, and never the creator's facts. The user's voice still comes from `creator-ikigai.md`.

Two kinds of example below:
- **Real posts** by Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes), credited, with the moves labeled.
- **Illustrations** written to show each formula cleanly. They are not real posts and their numbers are made up.

---

## Real posts

### 1. Philosophical observation with a gatekeeper list
Credit: Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes)

```
The most exciting thing about AI is the freedom it puts within reach of anyone, anywhere (aka, time rich!)

Opportunity isn't gatekept to people with engineering degrees, fancy résumés, or millions in funding.

Now, the greatest advantage is simply the courage to try.
```

**Moves:** a big claim in the first line with a casual aside that names the creator's theme. A list of three things people assume they need, then the real advantage. A short aphorism close.

### 2. Contrarian story with lived credibility
Credit: Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes)

```
A growth-focused product manager is the WORST person to become a content creator.

As a PM, I was trained to hinge everything on metrics and so naturally when I started to take content seriously, I focused on my nUmb3rS.

But it sucked the soul out of creating...
```

**Moves:** the hook is contrarian and carries the credential inside it (a PM saying PMs are bad at this). One all-caps word for the punch. A playful spelling to mock the thing being criticized. Ends on a cliffhanger so the reader wants the next post.

### 3. Proof story opener with stacked specifics
Credit: Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes)

```
So I'd been stuck at 1.5K followers on Instagram for ~10m

I was posting 5-8/week about tech, ai, startups, and how much Dancing with the Stars pays its cast. My algo was confused. Fair, I was too!

Then a friend who grew 0 to 100K told me his entire strategy was one word: FOCUS.
```

**Moves:** casual "So" opener. Several hard numbers written the way people text them. A quick self-deprecating joke right after the confession. A one-word reveal at the turn.

## Illustrations (not real posts)

### Pattern observation
```
A pattern I've noticed in people who solve hard problems:

They don't start by googling solutions.

They sit with the problem. Write it down. Break it apart. Ask better questions.

Only then do they look for answers.

Most people skip straight to searching for solutions to problems they haven't fully understood.
```

### Build-in-public update
```
Week 3 of building my content system:

Created 15 pieces this week. Took 6 hours total (used to take 20).

What changed: templates, written-down voice rules, and one checklist I run before posting.

Still refining it, but I can already see how it scales.
```

### Problem → solution
```
You can't focus because you don't have a priority.

When everything feels important, nothing is. Your brain doesn't know what to lock onto.

Pick one thing. Make everything else wait.
```

### Proof story (timeline)
```
My first 6 months freelancing: $0

Month 7: first client, a $500 project

Month 12: $3K/month, consistently

Month 18: $8K/month, booked 3 months out

It didn't click overnight. But once it clicked, everything moved fast.
```

### Contrarian statement
```
You don't need to hustle harder.

You need to sleep better, eat better, and stress less.

Everyone's optimizing for more output. Almost nobody is optimizing for better inputs.

Your energy sets your results more than your effort does.
```
