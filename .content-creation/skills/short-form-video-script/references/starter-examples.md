# Starter examples: short-form video scripts

Two parts:
- **Frameworks**: method. Read them every time, even after the user has winners.
- **Example arcs**: use only until the user has winners in `content/winners/short-form-video/`. Copy the beat structure, never the words. Adapt the beats to the user's topic, authority sources and stories.

---

## Frameworks

### Curiosity-gap arc
Short-form platforms reward full watch-through. Reveal the thing the viewer is curious about in the middle or at the end, not in the hook. Best for direct-to-camera videos whose hook is a surprising or counterintuitive claim.

1. **Hook**: a bold curiosity-gap statement ("This company is paying this job MORE than product managers").
2. **Tease** (TUTORIAL only; AUTHORITY scripts skip it): tell them to stay ("stick around, I'll tell you which one at the end"). It creates a contract.
3. **Build**: useful context, story or support. Don't reveal yet.
4. **Payoff**: answer the hook at or near the end.

Tease ideas: "Stick around if you want [payoff]." / "Turns out people don't like X. They like this instead." / "I'm going to share X. But first, some context." / "This happened. Let me give you the backstory."

**Why it works:** open loop + tease (staying feels like a choice) + delayed payoff. Use it only when the reveal is genuinely surprising.

### The story
1. **Hook**: stop the scroll.
2. **Backstory**: set the scene, make it relatable.
3. **Transformation**: the shift, the turning point.
4. **Takeaway**: the lesson they can apply.
5. **CTA**: what to do next.

### The permission trigger
**Insight:** validation beats information. People scroll for validation first: they want someone to tell them it's safe to try what they've been thinking about. Most creators teach tactics; the stronger move is to give permission.

1. **Name the thing they're afraid to try**: "Thinking about quitting your job to freelance?"
2. **Acknowledge why they're hesitant**: "Everyone says you need 6 months of savings first."
3. **Give explicit permission**: "It's okay to start with 2 months of savings if you have a plan."

**Permission audit (for ideas):** what does the reader want to try but feel they "should" wait on? What conventional wisdom holds them back? What would they do if failing carried no judgment?

### The belief stack
**Insight:** before someone follows or trusts you, they must believe three things in order. Miss one and the chain breaks. Jumping straight to belief 3 reads as bragging.

1. **This problem matters**: urgency, pain, cost of doing nothing.
2. **This solution works**: proof (numbers, before and after, how the method works).
3. **This person can help me**: authority plus relatability (the user's story, behind the scenes, a demo).

**Belief-stack audit (for ideas):** what problem does the reader underestimate? What proof does the user have? What story makes the user the right guide?

## Example arcs from published videos (@its.mikareyes)

### Sequoia thesis: "the next trillion-dollar company isn't software" (AUTHORITY · strong saves)
**Topic:** the next trillion-dollar companies sell outcomes, not software tools.
- Hook: on-screen "Sequoia: This is the next $1 TRILLION dollar company (not software)" / spoken "The investors behind Airbnb, Stripe, and Google said that the next trillion dollar company is not going to be software. It's going to be this instead."
- Tease the reveal ("this might change how you see AI companies").
- Stat drop: for every $1 spent on software, $6 goes to the people doing the work.
- Problem frame: most AI companies build tools that make professionals faster, and tools lose margin when better models ship.
- Pivot: outcome-first companies get better with every model improvement instead of getting disrupted.
- Concrete: you don't sell legal software, you sell the drafted contract.
- Reveal: the next trillion-dollar company looks like a services firm because it delivers the outcome.
- CTA: comment for the article link, then follow.

### Careers page salaries: "these jobs pay more than product managers" (AUTHORITY · strong shares)
**Topic:** marketing and community roles at a frontier AI lab pay on par with, or more than, product managers.
- Hook: talking head + screenshot overlay of the salary range / spoken "These jobs are getting paid more than product managers on Anthropic's Career Page."
- Personal credibility tied to the point: the creator used to be a PM, so she knows the baseline.
- Tension: engineers and PMs used to top the pay charts; something's shifting.
- Evidence: a live screen walk through the careers page, comparing ranges across roles.
- Insight: once AI makes building easier, distribution and brand become the hard part.
- Validation + CTA: if you're in events, community or marketing, your skills are worth more than you think.

### Mark Cuban: "don't chase tech jobs" (AUTHORITY · strong saves)
**Topic:** the big AI-era opportunity isn't joining a frontier lab; it's helping small businesses adopt AI.
- Hook: on-screen "Mark Cuban: DON'T chase tech jobs" / spoken "The billionaire Shark Tank shark, Mark Cuban, says do not chase traditional tech jobs. Chase this instead."
- Tease: he's pointing at one of the biggest opportunities of the AI era.
- Authority drop: cite where he said it (a podcast).
- Market size: millions of small businesses with no AI budget or expert.
- Historical analogy: in the internet boom, he walked into companies that had never seen a PC.
- Insight: the people who got rich weren't building the internet; they helped small businesses use it.
- Permission: you don't need a frontier-lab job to win this.
- Payoff: be the person who walks into a 10–50 person business and sets up AI in their workflows.
- CTA: comment for the video link, then follow.

### "Fire your personal trainer": a Claude health coach (TUTORIAL · high saves and comments)
**Topic:** part of a numbered "steal this AI workflow" series: an AI health coach connected to fitness and sleep trackers, turning the data into an adaptive plan.
- Hook: on-screen "Fire your personal trainer for a Claude Health Coach" + tool logos / spoken "It's time to fire your personal trainer and replace them with Claude."
- Series anchor: "part 13 of my series" (a recognizable franchise brings people back).
- How it works: the assistant pulls every device's metrics together and recommends training and nutrition.
- Personal proof: "If my sleep score is bad, the next day's plan is lighter." It makes "personalized" tangible.
- Demo: screen walk of the daily view, weekly view and plan.
- CTA: comment for the prompt and step-by-step guide, then follow.

### Stanford's STORM method: AI research blind spot (AUTHORITY → TUTORIAL payoff · 37% skip rate, strong saves and comments)
**Topic:** have the AI role-play five different experts at once to escape the single "majority view", run as 4 prompts in about 5 minutes.
- Hook: on-screen "Stanford proved AI research has a 60% blind spot (& fixed it)" / spoken "Stanford just proved that if you use AI for research, you have a blind spot. So they built a 4-prompt fix that catches everything you're missing."
- Problem frame: one prompt gives one perspective, the consensus view.
- Method: the AI role-plays five experts on your topic.
- Five experts, named fast, each with a distinct payoff: practitioner (what works day to day), academic (what studies say), skeptic (the strongest counter-argument), economist (who profits), historian (the pattern that already played out).
- The 4 prompts: perspective scan → contradiction map → synthesis → the AI peer-reviews its own work.
- Time contrast: 5 minutes versus 40–60 hours by hand.
- CTA: comment the keyword and what you'd use it for.

### "Stanford exposed the problem, Karpathy has the fix" (AUTHORITY · 58x outlier on the account)
**Topic:** research shows AI assistants agree with you far more than a person would; a named researcher's "LLM council" fix has several advisors attack an idea from different angles.
**Why it works:** two authorities doing different jobs: one makes the problem unarguable, one makes the fix aspirational. "Yes man" is instantly relatable. The blind-review detail makes the fix feel airtight, and the final ruling lands at the end, which pulls full watch-through.
- Hook (dual authority): on-screen "Stanford: exposed AI's problem / Karpathy: I have a fix" / spoken "It's official. Stanford just proved Claude is a yes man. It agrees with you 49% more than a human would. And Andrej Karpathy, founding member of OpenAI, built the fix." (one credential clause trimmed from the original)
- Problem proof: one more stat on how often it happens.
- Fix intro: name it, credit the person, describe it in one line (five advisors, each attacking from a different angle).
- Five advisors, named fast.
- Blind review: each advisor reviews the others with no names attached, so the idea is judged, not the person.
- Payoff: a chairman reads it all and gives the final call with next steps.
- Reframe close: from one yes-man to five sharp voices.
- CTA: comment the keyword to get the prompt.

## Arcs seen across outlier creators (no wording copied)

- **Big number + negation of the obvious.** Hook: "[skill] pays [specific $] right now, and it's not [obvious answer]." → stack of named, recent news sources as proof → root-cause reframe (why this is happening now) → misdirection ("you might think it's X, it's not") → name the skill → a numbered how-to → permission close (your unusual background is an advantage).
- **"Stop [old way], do [new way]."** Hook: named insider says they've stopped doing the thing the viewer just learned, with a screenshot of their post → the old way's pain → one-line definition of the new way → a small numbered set of building blocks → CTA keyword for the setup.
- **"Replace your [expensive team]."** Hook: "did you know you can run your entire [costly department] inside [tool]?" → one tool or skill per beat, each with what it replaces → close on old way (cost, weeks) versus new way (one afternoon) → CTA keyword for the install guide.
