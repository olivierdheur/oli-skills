# Platforms: carousels on Instagram and LinkedIn

Checked against official help docs, November 2026. Platforms change these often.

The method is the same on both: a cover that stops the scroll, one point per slide, one CTA at the end. What changes is the file format, the limits, and the text that sits next to the slides.

## Limits

| | Format | Limit |
| - | - | - |
| Instagram | Photos and videos, posted together as one feed post | Up to 20 photos and videos per post |
| LinkedIn | A document post: PDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC or DOCX | 100 MB and 300 pages per document |

This skill writes 7–10 slides, well under both limits. A higher cap is not a reason to add slides: it is the platform's limit, not a target.

## Instagram

- **What to export:** one image per slide, in order. The skill's design notes say what goes on each one.
- **Caption:** written by `short-form-video-caption` from the approved slides. Its own `references/platforms.md` has the caption limit and the comment-keyword CTA notes.
- **The cover is the feed preview.** Slide 1 is what people see before they decide to swipe, so it carries the hook and nothing else.

## LinkedIn

- **Post it as a document.** Export the slides as one PDF. LinkedIn accepts PPT, PPTX, DOC and DOCX too, but recommends PDF.
- **Flatten the PDF.** PDFs with multiple layers must be flattened or merged before upload, and every page must be the same size.
- **No motion.** Videos and animations in the document show as static images.
- **Add a title.** LinkedIn recommends a document title so members know what it's about. Use the cover hook or a plainer version of it.
- **It can't be edited after posting.** You can edit the post text or delete the post, but not the document. Proofread every slide before it goes up.
- **Viewers can download it.** Anyone who can see the post can download the document as a PDF, so don't put anything on a slide you wouldn't hand out.

## Posting one carousel to both

- Design once, export twice: images for Instagram, one PDF for LinkedIn.
- Write the Instagram caption and the LinkedIn post text separately. They do different jobs: the caption opens on the CTA, the LinkedIn text opens on the result or the tension.
- A comment-keyword CTA on the last slide needs a working way to send the resource on each platform. If it only works on one, use a save or follow CTA on the other version.
