# Starter examples: carousels

Used until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/visual/`. Once they do, their winners beat everything here.

**Every carousel below is illustrative.** The creators are fictional, and so are their numbers, clients and stories. Copy the structure, the slide discipline and the design-note style, never the words, and never borrow a number: the user's carousels use only facts from their own context files.

## The fictional creators

- **Jules Tran**, a physical therapist who helps people who sit at a desk all day fix their back pain in ten minutes a day. 1,200 desk-worker patients over eight years. Platform: Instagram. Comment keyword: `DESK`.
- **Priya Natarajan**, a fractional CFO who has closed the books for 40+ seed-stage startups. Audience: first-time founders who do their own finance. Platform: LinkedIn (document post).

## Cover hook patterns

- **Number + identity:** "5 desk habits wrecking your back (from 1,200 patients)". The number promises the size of the payoff; the subline carries the credibility.
- **Symptom question:** "Why does your back hurt more on Fridays?" The reader already feels it.
- **Before/after with a constraint:** "From 12-day close to 3 days. No new hires."
- **Contrarian claim:** "Stop stretching your lower back." It breaks a belief the reader holds, and the slides have to earn it.
- **"If you're [identity]":** "If you're a founder doing your own books, read this first."

## Example 1: Mistakes → fixes (Instagram, 9 slides)

Illustrative. Jules Tran. Pillar: desk-worker back pain.

```
SLIDE 1 (Cover)
**5 desk habits wrecking your back**
From 1,200 desk-worker patients
Design: Big bold headline, photo of a slouched person at a laptop, subline small at the bottom.

SLIDE 2 (Context)
**You don't need a new chair**
Most desk back pain comes from five habits, and all five cost nothing to fix.
Design: Plain background, header large, one line below.

SLIDE 3
**Mistake 1: Your screen is too low**
Your head drifts forward to read it. Raise the top of the screen to eye level. A stack of books works.
Design: Side-by-side line drawing, low screen vs eye-level screen. Same layout for slides 3–7.

SLIDE 4
**Mistake 2: You sit for 90 minutes straight**
Set a 30-minute timer. Stand for one minute when it rings. That's it.
Design: Timer icon with "30:00", header above.

SLIDE 5
**Mistake 3: Your feet don't touch the floor**
Dangling feet pull your pelvis forward. Put a box or a backpack under them.
Design: Same side-by-side drawing style as slide 3.

SLIDE 6
**Mistake 4: Laptop on your lap**
Your shoulders round to reach the keys. Use any table, even for 20-minute tasks.
Design: Same side-by-side drawing style.

SLIDE 7
**Mistake 5: Stretching only when it hurts**
By then the muscle is already guarding. Two minutes of hip openers every morning does more.
Design: Same side-by-side drawing style.

SLIDE 8
**Start with the screen**
It's the fastest fix and most patients feel the difference in a week. Do the other four after.
Design: Checklist of the 5 habits, the first one highlighted.

SLIDE 9 (CTA)
**Want my 2-minute morning routine?**
Comment DESK and I'll send it to you.
Design: Different background color from the value slides, keyword in large type.
```

**Why it works:**
- **The cover carries the credibility.** "1,200 patients" is on slide 1, not saved for later.
- **Headers alone teach.** Swipe only the bold lines and you have the five fixes.
- **One point per slide, 15–30 words each.** No slide restates another.
- **Slide 8 removes "this is too much".** It names the single easiest place to start.
- **Consistent value slides, distinct cover and CTA.** The design notes say so explicitly.

## Example 2: Before / after (LinkedIn document, 8 slides)

Illustrative. Priya Natarajan. Pillar: finance for first-time founders.

```
Document title: How to close your books in 3 days, not 12

SLIDE 1 (Cover)
**From a 12-day close to 3 days**
The four-step checklist, from 40+ startup closes
Design: Two big numbers, "12" struck through and "3" in accent color.

SLIDE 2 (Before)
**Day 12, and still no numbers**
Every hiring and spend decision waited on the close. The founder chased receipts by email.
Design: Calendar graphic with 12 days shaded.

SLIDE 3 (What changed)
**The close isn't slow. The inputs are late.**
Nine of the twelve days were spent waiting for documents, not doing accounting.
Design: Bar split into "waiting" (9 days) and "work" (3 days).

SLIDE 4
**1. Receipts go in the same day**
Card receipts are forwarded on the day of purchase, not at month end.
Design: Numbered step, same layout for slides 4–7.

SLIDE 5
**2. Bank feeds reconciled weekly**
Fifteen minutes every Friday instead of a full day at month end.
Design: Same numbered step layout.

SLIDE 6
**3. One owner per open question**
Every unexplained transaction gets a name and a deadline, in one shared list.
Design: Same numbered step layout.

SLIDE 7
**4. Close starts on day 1, not day 5**
With inputs already in, the first draft of the numbers exists on the first business day.
Design: Same numbered step layout.

SLIDE 8 (CTA)
**Save this for your next month end**
Follow for more finance for founders who do their own books.
Design: Plain background, short CTA centered.
```

**Post text (LinkedIn):**

```
Most founders think their month-end close is slow because accounting is hard. In 40+ closes, it was almost never the accounting.

It was waiting: for receipts, for answers, for someone to reconcile the bank. The checklist in this document moved one seed-stage team from 12 days to 3.

If you only do one thing this month, do step 1. It's the one that unblocks the rest.
```

**Why it works:**
- **The before is specific and honest.** Day 12, receipts by email: the reader recognizes it.
- **Slide 3 is the diagnosis.** It reframes the problem before giving steps, so the steps feel earned.
- **The post text adds what the slides don't:** the "it's the waiting" insight in plain words and where to start. It doesn't list the steps again.
- **The document title is a plainer version of the cover hook,** so it reads well in the feed.
