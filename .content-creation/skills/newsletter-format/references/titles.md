# Titles and subtitles

Write these once, at the formatting stage, from the finished body. Deliver **3–5 title + subtitle pairs**. The user chooses; never pick for them or apply one silently. Keep a title the user already chose.

## The title

A cold reader sees the title in an inbox, a feed or a social card before they know anything about the writer. The title carries the claim and the credibility itself.

**Ingredients for practical, how-to issues** (use the ones the article earns):
- **A number:** how many, how long, how much, what changed.
- **Proof:** first-person evidence the writer actually did this ("I", "my", a timeframe, a result).
- **The specific thing:** the named method, tool or artifact the issue is about.

**For essays and arguments:** lead with the specific tension, the conviction, or what the reader gets. A number and first-person proof aren't required. Never force them in.

**Working shapes:**
- `<N> <things> That <proof verb> <specific outcome>`
- `How I <outcome> in <time> with <named method or tool>`
- `The <N>-step <system> I <run / use> <frequency>`
- `I <did a specific thing>. Here's <what worked / what I'd change>`
- `<Outcome> (Without <the thing readers assume they need>)`

**Avoid:**
- Ending in a question mark.
- Colon-clever labels ("Productivity: The Next Frontier").
- Empty verbs: supercharge, unlock, transform, master, revolutionize. Plus anything banned in `creator-ikigai.md`.
- "Game-changer", "secret weapon", "you need to know".
- A number with no proof attached ("7 Tools": whose, used for what?).
- A promise the article doesn't deliver. A boring true title beats an exciting false one.

## The subtitle: use the space

**The subtitle carries a different idea from the title.** A restatement wastes the second most-read line on the page.

Pick the axis the title isn't on:

| If the title carries… | The subtitle carries… |
|---|---|
| The outcome | The method, naming the actual artifacts |
| A number or list | Who it's for, or the constraint it works under |
| A claim | The receipt: timeframe, cost, scale |
| A named tool or method | What it replaced, or what it costs |
| The result | The failure mode, or what the writer would do differently |

Mechanics: one sentence, no question mark, at least two concrete nouns from inside the article, and it doesn't repeat the title's main noun.

**Good pairing** (title = outcome, subtitle = method + artifacts):
> **How I Cut My Weekly Planning From 3 Hours to 40 Minutes**
> The one-page template, the Sunday checklist and the meeting I stopped taking.

**Bad pairing** (subtitle restates the title):
> **How I Cut My Weekly Planning From 3 Hours to 40 Minutes**
> Here's how I made my weekly planning much faster.

(Both are illustrations, not real titles.)

## Building the options

- Draw every number, name and timeframe from the draft.
- Make the options differ by angle: one leads with the number, one with the method, one with the outcome, one with the contrarian take. Two rewordings of the same idea count as one option.
- If nothing in the draft supports a number or proof point, say so and give the strongest honest options.

Deliver them like this:

```
1. **<Title>**
   <Subtitle>
   → title: <axis>; subtitle: <axis>
```
