# Editorial questionnaire, progression and review

## Collaborative discovery

The questionnaire is a shared editorial agreement, not a private checklist the agent fills in silently. Interview and brainstorm before choosing structure. Offer interpretations as proposals, explain what supports them, and let the user disagree. Practicality matters, but it should follow from the thought actually being developed.

## The completed questionnaire to show the user

Use a compact table or numbered list. For each answer include the proposed answer, its grounding in the interview, and its status (directly stated / proposed interpretation / open). Keep factual confidence separate from editorial approval. Ask explicitly whether these are the answers the user wants the article to express.

1. **Main takeaway.** What should the reader understand, believe or do differently? A specific claim, not a topic label. If several claims compete, show the alternatives and recommend one for discussion.
2. **Opening scene or entry point.** What real moment, observation, question or example opens the door? Don't invent dialogue or insist on a cinematic scene.
3. **Tension / reader question.** What makes this worth reading? Curiosity, a contradiction or an uncertainty all count. Don't manufacture conflict, suffering or stakes.
4. **Supporting ideas and evidence.** What experiences, examples or reasoning support the takeaway? Keep differences and counterexamples. List candidate material without fixing a section count or ordering it yet.
5. **Turning point or development.** What changes in the author's or reader's understanding? If no single turning point exists, say so; a gradual exploration is legitimate.
6. **Practical payoff.** What can a reader use in their own life? Name an actual decision, question, action, experiment or diagnostic distinction, and how the material earns it. Avoid vague inspiration and mandatory action lists alike.
7. **Ending.** What thought, feeling, possibility or open question should stay with the reader? Propose it for confirmation; don't default to gratitude, a callback or an engagement question.

After the seven answers, surface only the assumptions that matter: intended reader, limits of the claim, missing evidence, or what is deliberately out of scope. These are not another compulsory questionnaire.

**Stop here for confirmation.** A new anecdote does not approve your interpretation. Don't attach an outline as a bonus.

## Choosing a progression

Only after the questionnaire is confirmed, offer a few sentences per progression. Explain how the beats relate: why each thought follows the last and how understanding develops. Give materially different routes when they exist, recommend one, and let the user pick or adjust before detailed headings.

Possible forms include a reflective exploration, an argument built through examples, a candid Q&A, or a practical guide. None is the default. Structure follows the confirmed point and what the reader needs. Examples and usable advice can appear throughout rather than being split into a story block and a lesson block.

## Detailed outline and draft review

After the progression is picked, expand it into headings and evidence, and get the outline approved before drafting. Before delivering a draft, review it against that agreement:

- **Fidelity.** Does it express the confirmed answers? Did an interpretation become a fact, universal claim or new thesis without approval?
- **Progression.** Does each section develop the thought, rather than group notes under abstract labels?
- **Voice and texture.** Specific observations, natural humor, conversational reasoning and honest uncertainty stay in. Generic advice that could belong to anyone comes out.
- **Credibility.** Real experiences, accurate claims, supported examples. No invented conflict, dialogue, proof or causal claims.
- **Practical value.** Can readers apply the agreed insight? Are the examples concrete enough to make it usable? No quota of tips.
- **Audience and positioning.** Relevant to the reader in `persona.md` without forcing every theme from `creator-ikigai.md` into every piece. Verify external factual claims; reference essays are craft models, not factual authorities.
- **Economy and roadmap.** Normally 1,000–1,500 body words. **Here's what we cover** sits after the opening setup and before the body, and matches the real content. No redundant recap.

Resolve editorial problems inside the agreement. If a fix would change the agreed meaning, raise it with the user rather than rewriting the thesis silently.
