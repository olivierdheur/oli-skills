# Platforms: hooks for LinkedIn and long X posts

The hook is whatever shows before the "see more" fold, so the fold is the one platform detail that changes how a hook is written. The method doesn't change: full sentences, credibility or a contrarian claim, and a stated takeaway. Checked against official help docs, November 2026. Platforms change these often.

## LinkedIn

- **The fold.** The feed shows roughly the first two to three lines before "…see more" (commonly cited as about 200 characters on desktop, fewer on mobile; LinkedIn doesn't publish the number). Line breaks use up the visible space fast, so a hook spread over several short lines shows less of itself.
- **What that means for the hook.** The claim and the proof have to land in about the first 150 characters, so they survive on mobile. The promised takeaway can sit just past that if it has to, but the reader should know the topic before the fold.
- **What it rewards.** LinkedIn's engineering team has said its feed ranking uses dwell time, and creators commonly report that comments from the poster's professional network drive distribution. The "see more" click is commonly reported to count as a signal too.
- **Tone of the feed.** Professional identity is on display. People share what makes them look informed or generous to colleagues, which is why the shareability score leans on identity.

## Long X posts

- **Length.** Standard accounts are capped at 280 characters. Paid (Premium) accounts can post much longer, up to 25,000 characters.
- **The fold.** Long posts are cut off in the timeline with a "Show more" link, commonly reported at around the first 280 characters. The hook is everything above that cut.
- **What that means for the hook.** You have a little more room than on LinkedIn, but the opening sentence still does most of the work, because X readers scroll fast and judge on the first line. Lead with the number or the claim, not the setup.
- **What it rewards.** Creators commonly report that replies, reposts and bookmarks, especially in the first hour, drive reach. Bookmarks map directly to this skill's "built to be saved" goal.
- **Tone of the feed.** Blunter and faster than LinkedIn. A contrarian claim lands harder; corporate phrasing reads as an ad.

## Writing one hook for both

If the user posts on both, write the hook to the tighter fold (LinkedIn mobile): claim and proof in the first ~150 characters. It will then work on X as well. Adjust tone in the post, not the hook.
