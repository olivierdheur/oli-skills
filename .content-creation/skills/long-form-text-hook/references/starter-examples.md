# Starter examples: long-form text hooks

Used until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/long-form-text/`. Once they do, their winners beat everything here.

**Every example below is illustrative.** The creators are fictional, and so are their numbers. Copy the pattern, never the words, and never borrow a number: the user's hooks use only facts from their own context files.

## The fictional creators

- **Priya Natarajan**, a fractional CFO who has closed the books for 40+ seed-stage startups. Audience: first-time founders who do their own finance. Platform: LinkedIn.
- **Marco Bell**, a solo developer who grew a scheduling app to $18k MRR without hiring. Audience: indie hackers and developers with a side project. Platform: long X posts.

## Technique templates

Each technique has a template and an illustrative hook. The `Promises:` line is the takeaway the post has to deliver.

### Reader-focused

**Contradiction**
- Template: `[Thing everyone in your space does] is [the opposite of what they believe], and [proof].`
- "The startups with the prettiest financial models are usually the ones that run out of cash first. I've seen it in 9 of the 40 companies whose books I've closed, and the reason is one tab nobody builds."
- Promises: the one tab to add to a financial model.

**Stolen Thought**
- Template: `You [thing the reader privately suspects]. [Why it's true, with proof].`
- "You already know your SaaS pricing is too low, and you're keeping it there because raising it feels like asking for a fight. I raised mine 40% last spring, lost 3 customers out of 212, and here's the exact email I sent."
- Promises: the price-increase email, word for word.

**Direct Accusation**
- Template: `You're [specific behavior], and it's costing you [specific cost].`
- "You're paying for four finance tools that do the same job as a $20 spreadsheet, and the average seed-stage founder I work with is burning $640 a month on it."
- Promises: the audit for which tools to cancel.

**Absurd Reframe**
- Template: `[Mundane thing] is [dramatic framing], and [number that proves it].`
- "Your month-end close is a 12-day hostage situation, and the ransom is every decision you can't make until the numbers come back. Mine takes 3 days now, using a five-line checklist."
- Promises: the five-line close checklist.

**The Uncomfortable List**
- Template: `A list of [specific frustrations] that [narrow group] deal with, and the fix for each.`
- "A list of things nobody tells first-time founders about their first audit, from someone who has sat through 40 of them, with what to do about each one before the auditor shows up."
- Promises: the pre-audit list with a fix per item.

**Old Way vs New Way**
- Template: `[Process] used to mean [old, painful version]. Now it's [new version], and [proof].`
- "Bookkeeping used to mean a part-time hire and a two-week lag on every number. For the 11 startups I advise now, it's a bank feed, two rules and a 30-minute Friday review."
- Promises: the two rules and what the Friday review checks.

### Story-driven

**Buried Lede**
- Template: `[Mundane setup]. [The real story, with its number].`
- "I spent last month doing what I always do in January, which is cleaning up other people's spreadsheets. One of them was hiding a $90,000 sales tax bill the founder had never heard of, and the warning sign is in every startup's books."
- Promises: the warning sign and how to check for it.

**Contrarian Reset**
- Template: `If I had to [start over], I wouldn't [expected first step]. I'd [unexpected step], because [proof].`
- "If I were launching my app again, I wouldn't write a line of code before I had 50 people on a waitlist, because the version I built first, alone, for four months, got 6 signups."
- Promises: how to get the first 50 on a waitlist before building.

**Shame-to-Strength Flip**
- Template: `I used to be embarrassed that I [unconventional choice]. [What it got me, in numbers].`
- "I used to be embarrassed that I never raised money for my app. Two years later it pays me $18k a month and I still own all of it, and I'd make the same call for these three reasons."
- Promises: the three-reason test for whether to raise.

**Public Reversal**
- Template: `[Time] ago I said [strong take]. I was wrong, and [what changed my mind].`
- "Last year I told every founder I work with to skip a CFO until Series A. I was wrong for about a third of them, and there's one number that tells you which third you're in."
- Promises: the one number, and the threshold.

**PSA Norm-Breaker**
- Template: `PSA: I [decision that breaks a norm in the space], and [proof it worked].`
- "PSA: I stopped offering a free tier eight months ago, and my support tickets dropped by 70% while revenue went up. Here's how to decide whether yours should go too."
- Promises: the decision rule for killing a free tier.

### Authority-earned

**Authority Flex**
- Template: `I [did X under a constraint]. [The lesson the constraint teaches].`
- "I grew my app to $18k a month without a single paid ad, and 61% of that revenue traces back to one page most indie developers never build."
- Promises: what the page is and how to build it.

**Specific Number + Unexpected Context**
- Template: `[Specific number] + [context that surprises].`
- "I've reviewed 40 seed-stage cap tables, and the most common mistake isn't in the equity at all. It's a two-line clause in the SAFE that 14 of those founders had never read."
- Promises: the clause and what to do about it.

**Insider Confession**
- Template: `I've [credential]. Here's [the non-obvious thing insiders know].`
- "I've closed the books for 40 startups, and the founders with the cleanest numbers aren't the most organized ones. They're the ones who follow one boring rule about receipts, and it takes four minutes a week."
- Promises: the receipts rule.

**Credible Broker**
- Template: `I asked [N] [people] about [Y]. [The surprising pattern].`
- "I asked 30 indie developers making over $5k a month how they priced their first plan, and 22 of them did the same thing: they doubled the price they first wrote down. Here's the pricing worksheet they described."
- Promises: the pricing worksheet.

**Unlikely Source**
- Template: `[Unexpected person] taught me [lesson] about [topic], and [proof].`
- "My dentist taught me more about SaaS retention than any growth course, because his reminder system keeps 84% of patients coming back and I copied it almost step for step."
- Promises: the reminder system, step by step.

### Situational

**Process Metaphor**
- Template: `I [relationship verb] [N] [things]. [Filtering step]. [Outcome], and [the process].`
- "I went on first dates with 9 bookkeepers before I hired one. Six failed the first question, two failed the second, and the five questions I asked are now the only interview I'll run."
- Promises: the five interview questions.

**Ranked Decisions**
- Template: `Best decision I made this year: [personal]. Second best: [professional, with proof].`
- "Best decision I made this year was taking every Friday off. Second best was the automation that made it possible, which now handles 80% of my support inbox."
- Promises: the automation setup.

## A full worked output

Illustrative, for Priya on LinkedIn. Topic: founders who do their own month-end close.

```
Topic: Month-end close for founders who do their own books
Platform: LinkedIn
Pillar: Finance ops for first-time founders

1. (Absurd Reframe) Your month-end close is a 12-day hostage situation, and the ransom is every decision you can't make until the numbers come back. Mine takes 3 days now, using a five-line checklist.
   Promises: the five-line close checklist
   Shareability: 4/5 — names a pain every founder feels without being told; "hostage" gets forwarded
2. (Contradiction) The founders with the cleanest books spend less time on finance than anyone else, not more. Across the 40 startups I've closed, the difference was three habits that take under an hour a week.
   Promises: the three habits
   Shareability: 3/5 — useful, but "clean books" is not an identity anyone brags about
   → Make it shareable: name the reader's pride point, e.g. "founders who know their runway to the week"
...
Strongest: 1 and 2. Hook 1 has the clearest promise and the most forwardable line.
```

## What not to write

These fail the skill's rules. Each comes with the fix.

- "I wrote a post every day for a year and barely grew." No proof, no claim, no promise: a stranger complaining. Fix: add the number that changed and the takeaway. "I wrote daily for a year to 300 followers, then changed one thing about my first line and gained 4,000 in eight weeks. Here's the before and after."
- "Hot take:" alone on a line, then the claim. Colon-bait. Fix: say the take in a full sentence.
- "Stop doing this.\nDo this instead." The chopped two-line structure. Fix: one real sentence that names what "this" is.
- "Here's what nobody tells you about fundraising…" No proof and no promise. Fix: name the credential and the specific thing.
