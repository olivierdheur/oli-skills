# Starter examples: short posts from a newsletter

Use these only until the user has winners of their own in `content/winners/short-form-text/`. Copy the **move** (how an idea is lifted out of an issue, how the hook carries proof, where the link goes), never the words or the creator's facts. The user's voice comes from `creator-ikigai.md`.

Two kinds of example below:
- **One real post** by Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes), cut from one of her Substack issues, credited, with the moves labeled.
- **Illustrations** written to show the method. They are not real posts and their numbers are made up.

---

## Real post

Credit: Mika Reyes (@its.mikareyes), from her issue "How a 1-Hour AI Audit Got Me 653K Search Impressions (Without Being an Expert)"

```
I'm not an SEO expert.

But one AI-run audit on my site took my impressions up 300% in a week, and to 653K over the next 3 months.

Turns out most of the gains were low-hanging fruit I'd just never looked at :)
```

Link segment (final X post and final Threads item; last line of the Note):

```
<the user's link-back line>: <issue URL with tracking tags>
```

**Moves:**
- The idea is the issue's credibility beat, lifted out on its own. It needs none of the article's setup.
- An honest admission as the hook, then the proof right behind it: two real numbers and a timeframe from the issue.
- The close names the takeaway (most gains were easy fixes) without teasing the article.
- The link sits in its own final segment, so post 1 stands alone.

## Illustration: idea list from one issue

For a fictional issue titled "What 200 Client Calls Taught Me About Pricing":

```
1. The first-call question: asking budget before scope saved hours per lead
2. Contrarian: discounts attracted the clients who churned fastest
3. Number: 3 of 4 clients who said "too expensive" meant "not sure it works"
4. Exercise: rewrite your price page so the first line states the outcome
5. Before/after: hourly quotes vs. one fixed package
6. Story: the call where a client asked to pay more
```

Each line is one point that lands alone. "The three-part framework from section 2" would not make the list: it only works after the article's setup.

## Illustration: three versions of one idea

Idea 2, "discounts attracted the clients who churned fastest":

```
### 2a: Contrarian
Discounts didn't grow my business. They filled it with the clients most likely to leave.
Of the clients who signed on a discount last year, most were gone within three months.

### 2b: Proof story
I ran a 30% launch discount and signed 11 clients in a week.
By month three, 8 had cancelled.
The full-price clients from the same month? All still here.

### 2c: Direct advice
Stop discounting to close a hesitant client.
A discount answers "is it worth the price?" The real question is usually "will it work for me?" Answer that one instead.
```

Same facts, three angles. Every number would have to come from the issue itself.
