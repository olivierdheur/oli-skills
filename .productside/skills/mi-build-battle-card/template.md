## Thirty-Second Read

- **The wedge:** [Who they are and what they are pushing — and what that push costs this buyer. One sentence. This is framing, not description: the same facts a neutral summary would carry, arranged so a seller can use them. "They are moving hard into X, and the move is producing Y that this buyer feels in month one" beats "they are a credible vendor with a strong X offering." Both are true; only one is usable.]
- **When we win:** [the conditions, specifically]
- **When we lose:** [the conditions, specifically — this is not optional. A card that never says when you lose is fiction, and reps know it.]

The wedge is still evidence-backed and still honest about what is true of them. Reframing is not overclaiming: if the sentence would sit comfortably on their own website, it is a description and it is not doing a seller's work.

## Why Us, Structurally (max 3)

Claims about **you**, not the negative image of their problems. "Their pricing is unpredictable" is a warning; "your three-year cost is knowable at signing, here is the published schedule" is a reason to buy. Each needs an evidence row, and these come from the fusion brief's own-side rows — this run collects nothing.
